# Idea

I'm not entirely sure where the idea for Stack a Snack came from. It's a mix of everything that floats around in my head.

The idea took shape in my head before Falling Block Jam, and I didn't know this jam was a thing until I had gone into think mode (drawing dots and lines on a piece of paper).

*Insert paper drawing.*

So that this happened coincidentally was fun.

*I'll detour now.*

A bit sad about not being able to officially join the jam, they have a zero generative artificial intelligence policy (which I like, don't get me wrong). Fair enough. Since I don't know how to code a game like this, I used Claude to help me. I don't like this, but I like making games, and the alternative is to.. not, and I don't like that either. I don't know what I'm going on about, coding is hard, and using a generative articial intelligence is very easy, I'm lazy so that's that.

My point is that, it feels wrong to say that I participated in the Falling Block Jam. And I didn't, and I don't want to lie and say I didn't use generative artificial intelligence to assist me. So, I made this game alogside the jam. The jam pushed me, and I'm happy about that. Because without the push, Stack a Snack would not have been released as soon. I made it in roughly 6 days (not full days, I have kids, and a day job).

*My short detour ends here.*

And I had fun! What I like about games is making visuals, I like to draw pixels, and I enjoy designing mechanics, how the game works and flows.

Coding is mythical to me. I'm slowly learning. A goal of mine is to hand code a PICO-8 game on my own. Maybe my next idea! If I keep it simple enough, hehe. Oooo, maybe a [Sokoban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sokoban) inspired game! I've seen some nice tutorials for this on the PICO-8.

Anyway, I've always liked cake.

So for the falling block part, cake it is. Falling cake.

I also like bunnies. Who doesn't?

So that was the player and non player characters sorted. The big blue one is silly. I love that. I like wonky, uneven, different. I try to get that feeling in everything I make. I'm also happy with the simple animations, 2-3 sprites at most, it gets the point across and is lightweight.

# Sprite (pixels)

I drew all the game sprites in 8x8 pixels using [Pixelorama](https://pixelorama.org/), which is a nifty little pixel editing software. The label image is drawn in 128x128 pixels and imported into the label section.

Gray bunny (player) is made up of 9 sprites, 4 tiles. The tail and open mouth sprites are placed on the left or right side, depending on the direction you're facing and moving. I really like the expression of the open mouth, haha.

![Gray bunny](../images/posts/graybunny.webp "Gray bunny exploded and assembled view.")

Blue bunny (non player) is made up of 13 sprites, 13 tiles. Where 1 tile only show when chewing (right cheek, left on screen).

![Blue bunny](../images/posts/bluebunny.webp "Blue assembled views with alternating mouths open, closed, and chewing.")

Cakes (non player) are made up of 6 sprites (pink and blue variant), 3 tiles at most (in a 3 layer cake stack).

## Cake comparison

```lua
-- a stack (all(a)) only matches another stack (all(b)) that's identical to it piece for piece (all(counts)).
function same_stack(a,b)
 if #a!=#b then return false end
 local counts={0,0,0}
 for f in all(a) do counts[f+1]+=1 end
 for f in all(b) do counts[f+1]-=1 end
 for c in all(counts) do if c!=0 then return false end end
 return true
end
```

*Note on cake comparison: There's something off with this mechanic in game, sometimes the flying cakes don't match, gray bunny gets hit, blue bunny eats a mismatched cake, and you lose two hearts. This is super bad.*
